# Dialog zu Frage 2: Lord Henry und Jordan Belfort

Zurück zu [[Englisch/00 Start|Englisch]] · Gehört zu [[01 Dorian Gray – Discussion Chapter 2]]

> Are there any figures in popular culture whom you would compare Lord Henry to? Why?

Zwei längere Texte mit Belegen aus Kapitel 1 und 2: Aezri beginnt die Diskussion, Benedict schliesst sie ab. Darunter steht noch die erste Fassung als kurzer Dialog.

---

## Aezri (Einstieg)

Okay, so I'll start with question two. When I thought about who in pop culture is similar to Lord Henry, the first person that came to my mind was Jordan Belfort from *The Wolf of Wall Street*. I know he's way more extreme than Henry, but I think the way they influence people is actually really similar.

Belfort doesn't force anyone to do anything, he just talks. He gives these huge speeches in front of his employees, and after that they're all fired up and believe that money, luxury and partying are the only things that matter. And Henry does basically the same thing with Dorian, just in a garden instead of an office. In Chapter 2 he tells him "Live! Live the wonderful life that is in you! Be always searching for new sensations." When I read that, it honestly didn't sound like a normal conversation anymore. It sounded more like a motivational speech.

What I also noticed is that Dorian barely says anything back. He just listens, and Wilde even writes that the new ideas seemed to come from Dorian himself. I think it's exactly the same with Belfort's guys. They think they want this life, but actually Belfort put it into their heads.

And I have to add one thing that really surprised me. Henry himself says in Chapter 2 that influencing someone is immoral, because "to influence a person is to give him one's own soul." And then he does exactly that for the whole chapter. Basil even warned him in Chapter 1. He told him "Don't spoil him. Don't try to influence him. Your influence would be bad." So Henry knows what he's doing, and he still does it.

## Benedict (Abschluss)

Yeah, I agree with Aezri that Belfort is a good comparison, and I want to add a few things. First, both of them sell the same idea, that pleasure is the most important thing in life. Henry even talks about "a new Hedonism" and says that's what their century wants, and honestly, that could also be a sentence from Belfort. Both of them also care a lot about appearance. In Chapter 1 Henry says that he chooses his friends "for their good looks," which shows how shallow he is, and Belfort's whole world is about looking rich and successful too.

But I think there's also one big difference. Belfort ends up in prison, so in the end he pays for what he did. Henry doesn't. Basil says in Chapter 1 that Henry "never says a moral thing" and "never does a wrong thing," so Henry stays clean himself, and Dorian is the one who has to deal with everything later.

I wouldn't say that Henry is worse than Belfort, because Belfort really took people's money and Henry only talks. But I'd say Henry is sneakier. With Belfort everyone can see what he's doing because he's so loud. Henry is calm and charming, and that's why Dorian doesn't even notice what's happening to him.

So to sum it up, I think the main similarity is that both of them use words to pull young men into a life of pleasure and make it sound really exciting, without caring what happens to them afterwards. That's why I think Jordan Belfort is a really good modern example of Lord Henry.
