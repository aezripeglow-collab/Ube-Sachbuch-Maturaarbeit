# Dialog zu Frage 2: Lord Henry und Jordan Belfort

Zurück zu [[Englisch/00 Start|Englisch]] · Gehört zu [[01 Dorian Gray – Discussion Chapter 2]]

> Are there any figures in popular culture whom you would compare Lord Henry to? Why?

Gespräch zwischen zwei Personen, mit Belegen aus Kapitel 1 und 2. «Luca» ist ein Platzhalter für deinen Partner, den Namen einfach ersetzen.

---

**Aezri:** So for question two, I actually thought of Jordan Belfort from *The Wolf of Wall Street*. Have you seen the film?

**Luca:** Yeah, the one with Leonardo DiCaprio, right? The stockbroker guy. But why him? He's way more extreme than Lord Henry.

**Aezri:** Yeah, true, he's way crazier. But I think the way he works is actually really similar. Belfort doesn't force anyone, he just talks. He gives these huge speeches in front of his employees, and after that they're all fired up and believe that money and partying are the only things that matter. And Henry does basically the same thing with Dorian, just in a garden instead of an office.

**Luca:** Okay, but Henry doesn't give speeches to like a hundred people.

**Aezri:** No, that's true, it's only Dorian. But in Chapter 2 it really sounds like a speech. He goes "Live! Live the wonderful life that is in you! Be always searching for new sensations." That's not a normal conversation anymore, that's basically a motivational speech.

**Luca:** Hm, okay, fair. And Dorian doesn't really say much back, right?

**Aezri:** Exactly, he barely talks. He just listens, and Wilde even writes that the new ideas seemed to come from Dorian himself. I think it's the same with Belfort's guys. They think they want this life, but actually Belfort put it into their heads.

**Luca:** What I find kind of funny is that Henry even says in Chapter 2 that influencing someone is immoral. He says "to influence a person is to give him one's own soul," and then he does exactly that for the whole chapter.

**Aezri:** Yeah, honestly that surprised me too. And Basil already warned him in Chapter 1. He tells him "Don't spoil him. Don't try to influence him. Your influence would be bad." So Henry knows it, and he does it anyway.

**Luca:** But I think there's one big difference. Belfort ends up in prison, right? He pays for it in the end. Henry doesn't really do anything wrong himself.

**Aezri:** Yeah, that's a good point. Basil also says in Chapter 1 that Henry "never says a moral thing" and "never does a wrong thing." So Henry stays clean, and Dorian is the one who has to live with everything. I'd almost say that makes Henry worse, because he doesn't take any risk himself.

**Luca:** Hm, I'm not sure about worse. Belfort literally took people's money. Henry just talks.

**Aezri:** Okay, maybe not worse, but sneakier. With Belfort everyone can see what he's doing because he's so loud. Henry is calm and charming, and that's why Dorian doesn't even notice what's happening to him.

**Luca:** Okay, I can agree with that. So the main similarity is that both of them use words to pull young guys into a life of pleasure and make it sound super exciting.

**Aezri:** Yeah, exactly. Henry even talks about "a new Hedonism" and says that's what their century wants. Honestly, that could be a sentence from Belfort.
