## Part 1: Given on the exam sheet (Exam B)
You don't need to learn these by heart, but you must know **when** to use them.

| Formula | What it gives | Unit of the result | Use it when… |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $E = \dfrac{F}{q}$ | electric field from a force | **N/C** | a force on a test charge is given |
| $F = q\cdot E$ | force on a charge in a field | **N** | E is given, force wanted |
| $E = k\dfrac{Q}{r^2}$ | field of one point charge | **N/C** | a charge Q and a distance r are given |
| $B = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r} = 2\cdot10^{-7}\cdot\dfrac{I}{r}$ | magnetic field of a long wire | **T** | a current I and a distance r are given |
| $F = m\cdot a$ | force ↔ acceleration | N or m/s² | an acceleration is given or wanted |
| $F_G = m\cdot g$ | weight | **N** | "compare with its weight" |

**Constants:** $k = 8.99\cdot10^{9}$, $\mu_0 = 4\pi\cdot10^{-7}$, $e = 1.602\cdot10^{-19}$ C, $m_e = 9.109\cdot10^{-31}$ kg, $g = 9.81$ m/s²

## Part 2: Learn by heart (not on the Exam B sheet, but in the study problems)

| Formula | What it gives | Unit | Problem type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $F = B\cdot I\cdot L\cdot\sin\theta$ | force on a **wire** in a magnetic field | **N** | A1, A3 (rail gun) |
| $F = q\cdot v\cdot B\cdot\sin\theta$ | force on a **moving charge** | **N** | B1–B5 |
| $r = \dfrac{m\cdot v}{q\cdot B}$ and $B = \dfrac{m\cdot v}{q\cdot r}$ | **circle** of a charge in a magnetic field | m or T | C1–C4 (cyclotron) |
| $B = \dfrac{\mu_0\cdot N\cdot I}{L} = \mu_0\cdot n\cdot I$ | field **inside a solenoid** | **T** | E1–E3 |

**sin θ:** 90° → sin = 1 (biggest force). 0° or 180° (parallel) → sin = 0 → **no force**.

| Quantity | Letter | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| electric field | E | **N/C** |
| magnetic field | B | **T** (tesla) |
| force | F | **N** |
| charge | q, Q | **C** |
| current | I | **A** |

## Part 4: Directions

| Situation | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Field of a **+** charge | points **away** |
| Field of a **−** charge | points **toward** it |
| Force on a **negative** charge in E | **opposite** to E ("negative charges go against the field") |
| Wire through the page ⊙ (out) | field circles **counterclockwise** ↺ |
| Wire through the page ⊗ (in) | field circles **clockwise** ↻ |
| Wire in the page, current → | **above: out ⊙**, **below: into ⊗** (current ← : the other way round) |
| Force on wire / moving charge | 1. **Exclude** (F ⟂ v and ⟂ B) → 2. **Hand** (fingers along I or v, bend to B, thumb = F) or **circle** right → up → out → right → 3. **Negative? Flip!** |
| Bar magnets | outside **N → S**, strongest **near the poles** |

## Part 5: Recipes for the typical exam questions

**Two charges / two wires, field at the midpoint**
1. Distances in metres. 2. Each field separately. 3. Draw the arrows. 4. Same direction → **add**, opposite → **subtract**. Direction of the bigger one.

**Where is the field zero?**
- Same signs / same currents: **between** them, closer to the **smaller** one.
- Ratio: charges $\sqrt{Q_1/Q_2}$, wires $I_1/I_2$ (**no root**).
- **Blocks:** ratio + 1 blocks → one block = distance ÷ blocks → x = ratio · block.

**Electron in a field (Exam B Q4)**
F = e·E → direction (opposite) → weight m·g → compare (≈ 10¹³) → a = F/m.
Backwards (acceleration given): **F = m·a first**, then E = F/q.
